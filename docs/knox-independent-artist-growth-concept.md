# Knox Hill — Independent Artist Growth Concept

**Status: CONCEPT / NO SIGNED AGREEMENTS**

This document describes a speculative creative and promotional strategy for Knox Hill's current **D.B. Cooper** release and a possible future interactive experience.

It does **not** represent an agreement with Knox Hill, Ren, their representatives, labels, publishers, rights holders, or any other party. No collaboration, license, synchronization agreement, master-use permission, publishing permission, likeness permission, trademark permission, or endorsement should be inferred.

## The larger purpose

This is also a case study in an independent path:

> **Show independent artists how they can build an audience, tell a story, sell their work, and collaborate without assuming that the first step has to be signing a record deal.**

And for game developers who have been laid off or are between studio jobs:

> **Show how existing skills can be turned into owned creative work without losing the soul that made the work worth doing.**

The project is therefore both a potential Knox Hill campaign concept and a demonstration of an independent creator/developer workflow.

---

## 1. The Futur principle: make a living doing what you love

The Futur's stated mission is to teach one billion people how to make a living doing what they love **without losing their soul**. Its current "Our Story" page describes the mission as creating breakthrough moments for creative service professionals through content, coaching, and communities.

Source: https://thefutur.com/our-story

The page also uses a deliberately simple participation question:

> **What's your number?**

The current experience asks visitors to **Get My Number** as part of the 1 Billion Mission. As of September 25, 2026, the page displayed 33,572 of 1,000,000,000.

This project is not affiliated with The Futur. The useful idea is the framing:

> **Don't just talk about the mission. Put a number on participation.**

For this project, "What's your number?" can become a recurring prompt for independent creators and developers:

- What are you building?
- What do you need to make it sustainable?
- What can you sell without surrendering ownership?
- What can you teach someone else?
- What is the smallest working proof you can ship?
- What number of people do you need to support the work?

The point is not follower-count worship. It is making the economics and participation of independent creative work visible.

# 2. The Knox opportunity

The campaign should not reduce Knox to:

> "Rapper promoting a new single."

The unusual identity is:

**professional footballer → rapper → commentator / analyst → reaction creator → independent music artist**

That combination is the marketing opportunity.

The campaign should expose those existing identities rather than manufacture a new persona.

---

# 3. Core strategy: value → trust → transaction

The Chris Do / The Futur principle applied here is:

> **Give people something worth consuming before asking them to buy something.**

So the campaign should not become:

> D.B. Cooper is out. Buy it. Buy it. Buy it.

Instead:

> **Give people reasons to care about Knox Hill, then give them somewhere to go when they want more.**

Bandcamp Friday becomes a conversion moment inside a larger story.

# 15. The independent creator case study

The deeper lesson is:

> **You do not have to begin by asking someone else for permission to imagine the business.**

An independent artist can potentially build:

**story → audience → content → community → direct commerce → collaborations**

before deciding whether a larger commercial relationship makes sense.

A game developer can similarly combine:

**game development + web development + narrative design + research + interactive media + AI-assisted production + marketing strategy**

into an owned creative project.

The objective isn't to recreate a major studio.

It is to demonstrate that a small, coherent, useful artifact can be shipped and used as proof of capability.
